# PRD Quality Review — AgentDesk

Reviewed: `prd.md` and `addendum.md` (both dated 2026-09-05), cross-checked against the brief and brief addendum they cite. Calibration: hackathon MVP, five people, three build days, public judged demo with a platform ambition after the event. FR clarity is judged at launch level; length and ceremony at hackathon level.

## Overall verdict

This PRD is decisive, specific, and unusually testable for a hackathon document: the thesis in §1 is a real bet, every FR carries numbered consequences, Non-Goals do real work, and the §6.3 cut table makes de-scoping honest. What is at risk is the one beat the brief says wins the judges. UJ-4's slash-and-refund is specified against a real 60-second Binance ticker, which makes the outcome a coin flip and does not fit the three-minute script the UJs claim to mirror. Two engine-contract gaps (the skipped `execution` Node that lives only in the addendum, and who verifies and settles an x402 payment) will otherwise be resolved by architecture guessing. Fix those three and the PRD is ready for UX, architecture, and epics.

## Decision-readiness — adequate

Decisions are stated as decisions, not considerations: `execution` restricted to the platform (FR-14), no listing approval (FR-12), Stake minimum of ten times price (FR-7), Slash of exactly the Call's price (FR-35), Reputation over the last 30 scored Calls (FR-36), the platform holding every key (FR-2, Glossary "System Wallet"). §10 Open Questions are genuinely open and each names its fallback or its owner. The single `[NOTE FOR PM]` (FR-14) sits at a real tension.

Two tensions are dodged rather than surfaced. First, the PRD quietly picked one half of the brief's risk-table fallback for settlement in a short demo ("a short-window Binance ticker **or a mocked feed behind a demo-mode flag**") and then asserted an outcome that the chosen half cannot deliver (FR-44: "`research-sloppy` fails Settlement more often than it passes in demo mode"). Second, FR-34 scores the `risk` Agent on price direction, which is the `research` Agent's call, without acknowledging that a wrong LONG slashes both.

### Findings

- **high** Demo-mode Settlement is a coin flip, and the reproducibility claim rests on it (§4.8 FR-33, §4.10 FR-44, addendum §4, §6) — Sloppy Research returns LONG or SHORT "chosen against the 24h direction with confidence 0.9". Settlement in demo mode compares Binance `lastPrice` at Call time with the price 60 seconds later. A 60-second move on BNB/USDT is uncorrelated with the 24-hour direction, so P(fail) is about 50 percent, and the same holds for `research-good`. FR-44's consequence "fails Settlement more often than it passes in demo mode, so the refund beat in UJ-4 is reproducible" is therefore untestable as written, SM-1's "one Slash with Refund" depends on luck, and the brief's mocked-feed option (risk table row 4) was dropped without saying so. *Fix:* add a demo-mode setting to addendum §6 and FR-33 that the Settlement job uses a controllable reference price (a mocked feed or a demo-only "force outcome" flag on the job), tag it `[ASSUMPTION]` or `[NON-GOAL for MVP]` as appropriate, and state in FR-44 that reproducibility depends on it. If the team prefers the live coin flip with the recorded backup, say that in §7 Demo reliability instead of claiming reproducibility.
- **medium** Risk rule scores direction the `risk` Agent does not control (§4.8 FR-34) — "Settlement passes a `risk` Call whose decision was APPROVE or REDUCE if the resulting position's drawdown inside the Settlement Window stayed within 2 percent." On a spot market order, drawdown inside the window is dominated by price direction, which the `research` Agent chose. A sloppy LONG slashes both `research` and `risk` for the same event; a `risk` Agent that always REJECTs is never scored and never slashed. This is a real design tension with no `[NOTE FOR PM]`. *Fix:* keep the rule (it is simple and demo-able) but add a `[NOTE FOR PM]` naming the double-penalty and the REJECT-is-safe loophole, so the post-hackathon roadmap and the judge Q&A have an answer.
- **low** On-chain ownership is nominal while the platform holds the key (§4.2 FR-5) — "the identity is owned by the Creator, not the platform" is true on-chain but the platform signs with that key (FR-2, addendum §5). A judge will ask. *Fix:* one sentence in FR-5 or §8 Safety: ownership is on-chain and custodial in the MVP; external wallets are a Non-Goal (§5).
- **low** Run latency target does not fit the demo slot (§7 Run latency, §9 SM-3) — "under 60 seconds" and "at most 60 seconds" next to "The demo has 50 seconds per Run." *Fix:* set the requirement to the demo budget (45 seconds) or say the 60-second bound is the engineering ceiling and 50 the demo target.

## Substance over theater — strong

Nothing here is furniture. Two personas, both named in the UJs (Linh, Minh), and both drive decisions: the Builder's JTBD "never exceed my daily budget" becomes FR-3 and FR-20; the Creator's "be judged by results, and be able to top up and return" becomes FR-7 and FR-8. The Vision in §1 could not be swapped into another PRD: it names the three conditions and the one that is missing ("Marketplaces and rails exist; the consequence does not"). NFRs in §7 carry product-specific bounds (three retries, chain id 97, 10-second confirmation) rather than "scalable and secure". Differentiation is deliberately left in the brief and not re-performed here.

### Findings

- **low** Counter-metrics are scope guardrails in counter-metric clothing (§9 SM-C1, SM-C2) — "Number of Seed Agents or Types" and "UI polish" do not counterbalance the primary metrics; the claim that UI polish "Counterbalances SM-2 and SM-3" does not hold. The real counter-metric to "listing in 30 seconds with no approval" is junk listings, and to "zero manual approvals" it is money leaving wallets on a bad Call. *Fix:* either relabel SM-C1/C2 as scope guardrails under §8, or replace them with one honest counter-metric (payments made on Calls that later fail Settlement, or Listings that never pass FR-11).

## Strategic coherence — strong

The thesis in §1 ("a consequence when the agent is wrong") is the spine of the feature set: Stake, Price Lock, Settlement, Slash, Refund, and on-chain Reputation all serve it, and §6.3 orders the build so the spine ships first and the dashboard is cut first. The MVP scope kind is a platform proof (second-tab listing, stranger's wallet receiving money), and §6.1 matches it. SM-1, SM-4, and SM-5 validate the thesis rather than count activity; the "Post-hackathon signal" is correctly excluded from the MVP metrics.

Two places where the demo story and the spec pull apart:

### Findings

- **medium** The 60-second Settlement Window cannot land UJ-4 where the demo script puts it (§3 Glossary "Settlement Window", §2.3, §4.8 FR-33) — §2.3 says the journeys "mirror the three-minute demo script in the brief addendum §9". In that script the second Run starts at 1:50 and the slash lands at 2:20. With the research Call a few seconds into the Run, a 60-second window, the Settlement job, and a slash transaction to confirm, the slash lands after 3:00. *Fix:* set the demo-mode window to at most 20 seconds in addendum §6 and the Glossary, or restructure the script so the slashed Call comes from Run 1 and the window runs during the listing beat; then change "mirror" to "are adapted from" and note the deviation (the brief's "Risk rejects it" at 2:20 is also dropped, correctly, since Guardrail Risk only rejects above 6 percent volatility).
- **medium** Minh's live-listed Agent and the seed `research-sloppy` are the same beat told twice (§2.3 UJ-2, UJ-4; §4.10 FR-44) — UJ-2 lists a new Agent at 0.03 USDT; addendum §2 seeds Sloppy Research at 0.03 USDT; FR-44 says the seed exists "so the refund beat in UJ-4 is reproducible", but UJ-4 slashes Minh's agent, not the seed. Downstream cannot tell whether the demo lists the sloppy endpoint live (seed is the fallback) or lists a third research Agent. The closing line "The marketplace now ranks Minh's agent below the original research agent" also needs `research-good` to hold a Reputation above zero before the demo and a defined sort position for "no score yet" and "not scored in MVP" Listings (FR-13 is silent). *Fix:* state in FR-44 that Minh lists the Sloppy Research endpoint live and the seed Listing is the failover; add a demo-prep step (scored Runs for `research-good` before the demo) to §7 Demo reliability; add a sort rule to FR-13 (scored Agents by Reputation, then "no score yet", then "not scored in MVP").

## Done-ness clarity — adequate

This is the PRD's best-built dimension and still where the two most consequential gaps sit. Nearly every FR has a consequence an engineer can turn into a test: "within one second of sign-up" (FR-2), "shortfall shown" (FR-3), "both amounts shown" (FR-26), "within two seconds of each status change without a page reload" (FR-28), "one point per Settlement" (FR-42). Adjective-only conditions are rare ("clear message" in FR-1, "clear reason" in FR-32).

The gaps are in the engine contract that every Creator and every Seed Agent must implement, and in the Run state model that the Run view, the budget, and Settlement all depend on.

### Findings

- **high** The skipped `execution` Node exists only in the addendum (§4.6 FR-24, FR-29; §4.8 FR-34; addendum §1 "execution") — addendum §1 says "A `HOLD` signal or a `REJECT` decision means the `execution` Node is skipped as 'not needed', not failed." The PRD itself defines two Node outcomes: FR-24 "A Node starts only after the previous Node's Call succeeded" and FR-29 "A failed Node stops the Run". A skipped Node contradicts FR-24 (`notify` starts after a Node that neither succeeded nor failed), is invisible to FR-28's Run log and FR-39's Run status, leaves FR-3's reserved budget for `execution` unreleased, and is the only path to FR-34's "A `risk` Call with no resulting order is recorded as 'not scored'". The LONG-to-BUY / SHORT-to-SELL mapping in the same addendum paragraph is likewise engine behaviour absent from FR-24. *Fix:* add a consequence to FR-24: when `research` returns HOLD or `risk` returns REJECT, the `execution` Node is skipped with status "not needed", no payment is made, its reserved budget is released, and the chain continues to `notify`; state the side mapping there too. Add "skipped" to the statuses FR-28 records.
- **high** The x402 contract does not say who verifies and settles the payment, and FR-25's hash timing contradicts the addendum (§4.4 FR-16, §4.6 FR-25, §7 Openness, addendum §3) — FR-16 requires an Agent to answer "a request carrying a valid Payment Proof with HTTP 200" without saying how the Agent knows the proof is valid. Addendum §3 chooses a facilitator-settled authorisation ("permit2-exact scheme is expected with the Binance B402 facilitator"). Under that scheme the resource server verifies and settles through the facilitator, and the transaction hash exists at settlement, after the paid retry; FR-25's consequence "The payment tx hash is recorded on the Call before the retry is sent" describes the opposite flow, where the engine broadcasts a transfer itself. Downstream cannot build the six Seed Agents, the public contract page (FR-15), or meet §7 Openness ("from the public schema page and the HTTP contract alone") without knowing which flow is in force and what facilitator endpoint a third-party Creator uses. *Fix:* pick one. Either (a) facilitator flow: FR-16 gains "the Agent verifies and settles the Payment Proof through the platform-published facilitator URL", FR-25's hash consequence becomes "recorded when the settlement response arrives", and the facilitator URL is added to the public contract page; or (b) direct-transfer flow: say the engine broadcasts a USDT transfer and the Payment Proof is the tx hash, and rewrite addendum §3 to match. Hand the choice to architecture only if both variants are written out.
- **medium** Stake minimum is enforced at listing only, then referenced as if continuous (§4.2 FR-7, FR-8, FR-9; §4.8 FR-35) — FR-7 refuses a Listing "if the Stake is below ten times the price per call". FR-9 lets a Creator change price "at any time" with no Stake check, so a Creator can list at 0.01 with 0.1 Stake and reprice to 1.00. FR-8 resumes "when Stake is topped up above the minimum" without saying which price the minimum is computed from. FR-35's partial-slash branch ("If Stake is lower than the price") only exists because of this gap. *Fix:* state the rule once in the Glossary "Stake" entry: the minimum applies continuously; a price change that would put Stake below ten times the new price is refused (FR-9), and "the minimum" in FR-8 is ten times the current price.
- **low** No operator surface for the controls the PRD relies on (§3 "Emergency Stop", §4.7 FR-31, §4.3 FR-14, addendum §6) — Emergency Stop is "a platform-wide flag" and demo mode is an environment setting, but no FR says who sets either, and "the platform Account" in FR-14 is not defined. A story writer has nothing to hang the toggle on. *Fix:* one short FR under §4.7 or §8: the operator sets Emergency Stop and demo mode through configuration (env var or a single admin page), and the platform Account is a named seeded Account.
- **low** Price history is displayed but never recorded (§4.9 FR-42, §4.2 FR-9) — FR-42 shows "price history"; FR-9's consequences do not record a change event. *Fix:* add to FR-9: each price change is recorded with timestamp and old and new price.
- **low** Adjective-only conditions (§4.1 FR-1 "refused with a clear message", §4.7 FR-32 "fail with a clear reason") — *Fix:* name the message content ("email already registered"; "no Telegram chat id linked").

## Scope honesty — strong

Omissions are explicit and load-bearing. §5 rules out the things a judge or an engineer would otherwise assume in: third-party `execution`, platform fee collection, withdrawals, dispute handling. §6.2 states deferrals with the reason ("the demo uses two prepared Accounts"). §8 Time commits to cutting "per §6.3, not deferred silently". Six `[ASSUMPTION]` tags, six Open Questions, one `[NOTE FOR PM]`: an open-items density that fits a three-day build and does not block a green light.

### Findings

- **medium** One shared Sub-account means Builders have no exchange capital of their own, and the PRD never says so (§3 "Sub-account", §4.6 FR-24, §4.1 FR-4) — the Glossary defines "The Binance Spot Testnet sub-account" in the singular, and FR-24 feeds `balance_usdt` "from the Sub-account". So every Builder's orders go through one platform-owned exchange account, the Order Cap bounds platform capital rather than Linh's, and two Builders share one balance and one exchange-side limit. This is the right simplification for a testnet demo and the obvious judge question ("whose money is that order?"). *Fix:* add a `[NON-GOAL for MVP]` line in §5 or a `[ASSUMPTION]` in §4.7: all `execution` Calls trade one platform Sub-account; per-Builder exchange accounts and capital are post-hackathon.
- **low** FR-17 narrows the brief's workflow shapes without saying so (§4.4 FR-17, brief addendum §6) — the brief lists `data -> research -> execution` and two-`data` chains as shapes "the engine must accept"; FR-17 requires `risk` before `execution` and "at most one Node per Type". Both are good calls (the first is a safety rule). *Fix:* one clause in §5 or FR-17: "Chains from brief addendum §6 that skip `risk` or repeat a Type are out of scope; `risk` is mandatory before `execution`."
- **low** Whether a Refund restores the Daily Fee Budget is unstated (§4.1 FR-3, §4.8 FR-35) — FR-3 counts spend at Price Lock time and releases unpaid Nodes; a slashed-and-refunded Call is paid and later returned. *Fix:* one consequence in FR-3 or FR-35 either way.

## Downstream usability — strong

The Glossary in §3 is complete and used consistently in the FRs (Provider, Price Lock, Payment Proof, Settlement Window all appear with the defined meaning). FR-1 to FR-44 are contiguous and unique; every UJ names its protagonist and lists the FRs it realizes; every cross-reference resolves, including "brief addendum §9" and "§7" from Open Question 1. Each §4 group opens with a description that stands alone. UX, architecture, and epics can source-extract from this without reading the brief.

### Findings

- **medium** The addendum carries normative rules that the PRD presents as "field types and JSON examples" (§4.4 FR-15, §4.6 FR-24, addendum §1, §4) — beyond the skipped-Node rule above, addendum §1 fixes "`size_usdt` must be at most `proposed_size_usdt`; for `REJECT` it is `"0"`", "`reason` is a non-empty string, at most 500 characters", and "Unknown fields in a response are ignored; missing required fields fail validation"; addendum §4 fixes the reference-price sampling and "stored as basis points". These are acceptance criteria, and a story writer who takes FR-15's pointer literally will miss them. *Fix:* one sentence in §0 and FR-15: "Addendum §1, §3, and §4 are normative; the PRD's schema block is a summary." Or lift the three validation rules into FR-15's consequences.
- **low** "builder" the screen collides with "Builder" the role (§2.3 UJ-1 "The builder shows", §4.1 FR-3 "shown in the builder", §4.5 FR-19 "The builder validates") — the Glossary reserves "Builder" for the Account role. UX needs a name for the screen. *Fix:* add "Workflow Builder" (the editing screen) to the Glossary and use it in those four places.
- **low** The verification Call has no home, and two wallets are undefined (§4.3 FR-11, §4.6 FR-28, §4.9 FR-40) — FR-11 says the verification Call "is logged like any Call (FR-28)", but FR-28 is per-Run and FR-40 shows payments the Builder made; the verification payment is made by "a platform wallet", which is not in the Glossary, and neither is "the platform Account" (FR-14). *Fix:* add "Platform Wallet" and "Platform Account" to the Glossary; state that verification Calls appear on the Agent detail page (FR-42) rather than in a Run.

## Shape fit — strong

Chain-top PRD for a two-sided marketplace with a meaningful UI, feeding UX, architecture, and epics: the UJ-with-protagonist shape is load-bearing here and is used, with four journeys rather than a UJ per FR. The document does not repeat the brief's landscape, business model, or risk table, and says so in §0. Forty-four FRs is a lot for three days, but each is a few lines, and the §6.3 table turns the count into a cut list rather than a backlog. No section reads as template-driven; §8 Constraints and §9 counter-metrics are the closest and are short.
